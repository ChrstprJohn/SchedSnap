# Landing page refinement

The `/` surface uses **Persuade** mode: introduce UniToolbox briefly and help students reach an available tool. This is a scoped refinement of the incumbent interface. `PRODUCT.md` and `DESIGN.md` remain authoritative and unchanged; this record does not establish a new global visual system.

The page retains the white background, slate foreground, muted supporting text, quiet dividers, DM Sans Variable, shared page width, and primary-button treatment. A bespoke nine-module SVG pattern extends the header's Blocks logo with interlocking rounded geometry. Sage in the illustration carries into a restrained pale-sage stats strip; these colors remain local to the landing page.

The hero pairs “A little help for university life.” with “Student tools. Less busywork.” and one primary **Explore tools** anchor to the services section. The hero occupies the initial viewport; an at-a-glance banner and **Tools for student life** follow below it. Roadmap promises, the availability badge, the service icon, and the footer tagline remain removed. The footer keeps only copyright and the platform name.

The banner presents three metrics: total visits of **1,284**, visibly labelled **Sample**, plus **1 tool** and **36 wallpaper styles** derived from the service and template catalogs. Visits are a display fixture requested for preparing the design, with no analytics tracking or backend. `StatsBanner` accepts `visits` and `sample` props for a future real source; a null visit value displays an unavailable dash.

The featured service is one fully linked preview-and-copy row with a concise description and a prominent **Open tool** action. Three actual canvases render neutral example schedules in **Bear Mode**, **Window Light**, and **Daily Panda**, with the caption **Sample wallpapers**. Larger previews, a lifted center image, gentle outer rotations, and soft shadows give the exported artwork depth. `ServicePreview` reuses the tool's existing `WallpaperCanvas` renderer and is lazily imported to split its bundle. Canvas drawing waits until near the viewport when IntersectionObserver is available.

## Responsive, motion, and accessibility decisions

- The hero uses `min-height: calc(100svh - 4.5rem)` below the navigation, with vertically centered content. The navigation's divider puts the banner just beyond the initial screen: at 720.8px in the 720px-high desktop capture and 844.8px in the 844px-high mobile capture. A minimum height lets content grow on short screens or with enlarged text without clipping.
- Desktop uses a two-column hero, with copy on the left and the illustration on the right. The featured service uses two equal columns, preview on the left and copy on the right. At 900px and below, hero spacing tightens and the service column gap reduces to 32px.
- At 640px and below, the hero and featured service stack into single columns. Hero copy precedes the centered illustration, capped at 280px and 78% width; service previews precede their copy. The metrics retain three columns with smaller type and padding. At 480px and below, the existing page wrapper supplies 16px side gutters. Headline sizes scale with the viewport.
- Hero entrance motion uses short vertical offsets and a small illustration rotation, enabled only when `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` matches. GSAP media cleanup reverts on unmount. The existing reduced-motion rule disables transitions, including the service arrow's hover shift, and uses automatic scrolling.
- Sections have named headings; heading order is h1, h2, h3. The metrics use a labelled section and description list. Explore tools remains a native anchor and the service remains a router link named by its heading. The illustration has empty alternative text and is hidden from assistive technology; action arrows are decorative too. Preview canvases have image roles and labels naming the template and sample classes; loading has a status message and rendering failures have an alert. Shared visible focus outlines remain in use, and primary/action treatments provide a minimum 44px height. The SVG and canvases have intrinsic dimensions to reserve their aspect ratios.
